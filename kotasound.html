SpiroSound™ Technology

Kota Sound portable speakers feature our groundbreaking SpiroSound™ technology. This innovative design enhances sound quality by minimising internal reflections and eliminating unwanted resonances within the speaker enclosure.

The SpiroSound™ technology works by effectively dissipating sound waves inside the speaker cabinet, preventing them from interfering with the direct sound produced by the speaker driver. This results in a clean, precise audio output with minimal phase distortion.

Design render.

Projection and Directionality

The Kota speaker also features a unique, magnetically attached outer casing that further improves sound projection and directionality. This variable-tuned enclosure optimises the speaker's performance across the entire frequency range, ensuring a balanced and accurate sound reproduction.

By reducing turbulence in the sound waves, the Kota speaker achieves an impressive projection distance while maintaining clarity and coherence. This means you can enjoy high-quality audio at lower power levels, as the sound travels efficiently through the air without losing its definition.

With its combination of advanced SpiroSound™ technology and innovative design features, the Kota portable speaker delivers exceptional audio performance in a compact, energy-efficient package.

Computer-generated view of the design.

Two Casings, One Instrument

The Kota range of speakers consists of two parts: an inner casing housing the speaker driver and our patented wave dissipation technology, and the outer casing, with our patented beam focusing technology.

The relationship of the two casings together forms a variably tuned enclosure that has a balanced disposition to the frequency range. Due to the high efficiency of these enclosures it is possible to reduce the amount of power required to match the output of a traditional system, where much of the sound is lost due to its unfocused beam placement.

The small unit with flat ends is perfect for resting on a low coffee table, on its curved face, or on the ground outside, where it sends the short-throw sound at a forty-five degree angle towards the listeners. On a bookshelf, a mantelpiece or a wall outside, the sound sits at chest height.

Placed back into the continuation of the curve that is the larger casing, it suits large spaces and the great outdoors. With the combined shapes together, the sound propagates from a letterbox-shaped opening as a long-throw, directional beam: wide in one plane and narrow in the other.
